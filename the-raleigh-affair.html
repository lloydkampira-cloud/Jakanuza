The Raleigh Affair

By Lloyd Kampira

In Limbe, everyone has a story to tell, and sometimes an ordinary day can become a memorable one.

Mr. Nyalugwe was a bricklayer known for two things: unmatched skill with bricks and a close friendship with alcohol. People said his hands could raise a house faster than most men, but his habits made many employers wary. Five years ago his wife had had enough of the irresponsibility that came with drinking, and she left. From then on, Nyalugwe lived alone in a small house with only the basics: two pots, one plate, Mbaula, a thin mattress, some clothes, and a few items too humble to attract thieves.

But among all his belongings there was one treasure—a Raleigh bicycle. It was the love of his life, his world. He had bought it cheaply from Mzungu, one of his regular bosses, but it was still in perfect condition. Bwana Hetherwick had insisted Nyalugwe pay something for it, so that he would value it enough not to exchange it for a bottle of Ambuye Ntengeni wine. It was a paid gift, and Nyalugwe guarded it with pride.

One morning, as usual, Nyalugwe rode his bicycle to Limbe in search of piecework. He headed straight to the Pacific Tower Building, popularly known as kwa Faiza, where construction was ongoing even though the two lower floors were already operating.

As he stood there, a smart-looking man approached him, studying his appearance. The man introduced himself as the foreman and said the job was available; Nyalugwe only needed to sign a few papers. Relief washed over him. The foreman said they needed to go to his office, but the bicycle could not go with them and would be kept safely in one of the rooms of the building.

Nyalugwe watched as the man climbed the stairs with the bicycle, greeting people confidently, like someone who truly belonged there. He opened a room, entered, and closed the door. From outside, Nyalugwe saw everything and waited patiently for the man to come back so they could go to the office.

One hour passed. Then another. Something began to feel wrong.

Nyalugwe finally walked to the door where the man had entered and knocked. No response. He pushed it open, only to find that the room led straight to the building's back exit corridor. His heart sank. He waited two more hours, clinging to hope, until the truth became clear… he had been robbed.

That day Nyalugwe went home with no job and no bicycle…only dust, regret, and the painful lesson of trust. Meanwhile, business in Limbe Market continued loudly, as if nothing at all had happened.
