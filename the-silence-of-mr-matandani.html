The Silence of Mr. Matandani

By Lloyd Kampira

Some people are known for their laughter. Others are remembered for their silence. In Bangwe, Mr. Matandani had built quite a reputation for being a man who rarely showed emotion. But even the most serious people have their moments.

Smiling is a small thing, but it carries big power. A smile can open a closed heart, it can cool anger, invite friendship and make a heavy day feel lighter. Sometimes a smile is like sunshine after a long night of rain. It costs nothing, yet it gives so much.

But not everyone understands this simple secret.

Do not be like Mr. Matandani of Bangwe.

He was known for many things in the township, but smiling was not one of them. His face was always tight, like a door locked with three padlocks. Even children in the neighbourhood said, “If you see Mr. Matandani walking, hide your jokes.”

Life had shaped him that way.

Long ago, he worked hard at the railway workshop in Limbe. He was a strong man with hands like iron. But life dealt him some heavy blows. His wife died early, friends moved away and his business failed. Slowly, laughter left his house like smoke escaping through the roof. From that day, Mr. Matandani decided that smiling was for fools.

Years passed.

His grandchildren grew up visiting him every weekend. The children were different from him. They were full of jokes, noise and endless laughter. They would run around his yard like chickens that had just escaped the stainless knife.

But Mr. Matandani would sit quietly on his wooden chair, watching them with the same old serious face.

One afternoon, something strange happened.

The grandchildren started telling jokes about goats that behave like teachers and teachers who behave like goats. One child even imitated how Mr. Matandani used to shout when he was younger. The yard exploded with laughter.

Mr. Matandani tried to resist. His lips fought hard like soldiers refusing to surrender. But the jokes kept coming.

Then suddenly… it happened.

His mouth slowly opened.

A smile appeared.

Not a small one… a big one.

The grandchildren froze. The whole yard looked at Mr. Matandani as if the sun had decided to rise from the west.

Inside his body, the organs were also confused. The lungs whispered, “What is happening today?”

The stomach said, “Is this the same man who has been serious for thirty years?”

The throat, shocked by this sudden festival of laughter, forgot its normal duty.

Poor Matandani laughed so hard that he started choking. Yes, choking!

The grandchildren rushed around him. By the time help came, the old man had already gone quiet.

Bangwe learned an important lesson that day.

Smiling is good.

But if you refuse to smile for years, the first one might shock the whole system.

Smile today, tomorrow and often.
